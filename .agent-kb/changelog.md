- 2026-10-31 — chess — **The sleep trigger was wrong and is replaced.** `PMIC_IRQ` in `/proc/interrupts` counts every power-chip interrupt (charger, RTC, USB wake, the wake itself): 94 bumps for one press on 2026-10-31, and the old log showed `power button pressed (180 -> 181)` with nobody near the device — spurious sleeps mid-game. Counting `PWRON1` lines instead is not enough either (the 16 KB ring turns over in minutes here, so an insert plus an eviction cancels out). New: `klogctl(SIZE_BUFFER)` + `klogctl(READ_ALL)` into two buffers, `pwr_log_grew()` works out exactly how many bytes were written since the last look by finding the older copy's tail in the newer one, and only those bytes are searched. `/dev/kmsg` is unusable on this kernel (all reads `EINVAL`, proven by new `tools/kmsgprobe.c`); `klogctl(2)` is avoided because it consumes the shared log and would destroy `dmesg`. Selftest covers the shift and the cancellation. Deployed; press not yet confirmed on the panel.

- 2026-10-31 — chess — Power button now sleeps and wakes the Kindle, **confirmed on the panel**. The key turned out not to be an input device at all: `tools/keyprobe.c` watched event0..event4 through 90 s of presses and saw zero key events (event2 `whitney-button` only carries touch codes), and `/proc/whitney_button` never changes — it is the screen-saver lock flag.
